B.E.P. Bible Quiz

Test your understanding of God's Word through challenging questions that require biblical knowledge, interpretation and careful reading.

20 Questions • 60 Marks • Pass Mark: 40

Quiz Information

This B.E.P. Bible Quiz is designed to test more than simple Bible memorisation. Some questions require you to compare passages, understand biblical context and identify the theological meaning of a passage.

20 Questions
60 Total Marks
40 Pass Mark

Important: Each question carries 3 marks. Select one answer for every question.

B.E.P. Member Details

QUESTION 1 — 3 MARKS

According to Romans 4, why does Paul use Abraham as an example when explaining justification by faith?

QUESTION 2 — 3 MARKS

In Exodus 19:5–6, Israel is described as a “kingdom of priests and a holy nation.” What is the most appropriate meaning in the covenant context?

QUESTION 3 — 3 MARKS

Jesus' response in Mark 12:28–31 combines Deuteronomy 6:4–5 with Leviticus 19:18. What principle is most clearly demonstrated by this combination?

QUESTION 4 — 3 MARKS

Isaiah 53 describes the Servant as being “pierced for our transgressions” and “crushed for our iniquities.” What central idea does this passage communicate?

QUESTION 5 — 3 MARKS

Jeremiah 31:31–34 speaks of a “new covenant.” According to the passage, what distinguishes this covenant?

QUESTION 6 — 3 MARKS

Hebrews 7 uses Psalm 110 to explain Jesus' priesthood. What makes Christ's priesthood different from the Levitical priesthood?

QUESTION 7 — 3 MARKS

In Daniel 7:13–14, the “Son of Man” comes with the clouds and receives dominion, glory and a kingdom. What is significant about the vision?

QUESTION 8 — 3 MARKS

In Matthew 5:17–20, Jesus says He came not to abolish the Law or the Prophets but to fulfill them. Which interpretation best fits the immediate context?

QUESTION 9 — 3 MARKS

In John 15, Jesus describes Himself as the vine and His disciples as branches. What does “abiding” in Him primarily communicate?

QUESTION 10 — 3 MARKS

Romans 6 argues that believers should not continue living in sin because they have been united with Christ. What is Paul's main reasoning?

QUESTION 11 — 3 MARKS

According to Romans 8:15–17, what evidence of the Spirit's work is connected with believers being God's children?

QUESTION 12 — 3 MARKS

Paul's argument in 1 Corinthians 12 about spiritual gifts and the body of Christ is primarily intended to correct what kind of attitude?

QUESTION 13 — 3 MARKS

Galatians 2:16 says that a person is not justified by works of the law but through faith in Jesus Christ. What contrast is Paul emphasizing?

QUESTION 14 — 3 MARKS

Ephesians 2:8–10 teaches that salvation is “by grace through faith” and “not from works,” while also saying believers are created for good works. How should these statements be understood together?

QUESTION 15 — 3 MARKS

Philippians 2:5–11 presents Christ as one who humbled Himself and became obedient to death. What movement does the passage describe?

QUESTION 16 — 3 MARKS

Colossians 1:15–20 describes Christ as the one through whom all things were created and in whom all things hold together. What central claim about Christ is Paul making?

QUESTION 17 — 3 MARKS

Hebrews 4:14–16 calls Jesus the great High Priest who has been tempted in every way as we are, yet without sin. What encouragement does this provide?

QUESTION 18 — 3 MARKS

James 2:14–26 discusses faith and works. What is James specifically challenging?

QUESTION 19 — 3 MARKS

1 Peter 2:9 calls believers “a chosen people, a royal priesthood, a holy nation.” What purpose does Peter immediately associate with this identity?

QUESTION 20 — 3 MARKS

In Revelation 21:1–4, what is the significance of the “new heaven and new earth” and the statement that God will dwell with His people?
